## Why storage matters

Tea absorbs moisture, odors, and light. Poor storage dulls flavor and can introduce staleness or off-tastes within weeks. Proper conditions slow these changes and keep each cup closer to the tea's original character.

## Ideal storage conditions

| Factor | Target | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Humidity | 40–60 % relative humidity | Keeps leaves dry enough to prevent mold yet not so dry that they become brittle and lose volatile aromas |
| Temperature | Below 25°C / 77°F; refrigerate for long-term storage | Heat accelerates oxidation and flavor loss |
| Light | Complete darkness or opaque containers | UV and visible light break down chlorophyll and catechins, fading color and taste |
| Air exposure | Minimal; reseal after every use | Oxygen drives oxidation; ambient odors transfer into the leaves |

## Control humidity

Humidity is the single largest risk to stored tea. Follow these guidelines to manage it.

1. **Measure your storage area.** Use a small hygrometer to confirm relative humidity stays between 40 % and 60 %. Kitchens and bathrooms often exceed this range.
2. **Choose a dry, stable location.** A cupboard or pantry away from the stove, sink, and dishwasher avoids steam and temperature swings.
3. **Use desiccant packets when needed.** Place a food-safe silica gel packet inside the storage container if your environment regularly exceeds 60 % relative humidity. Replace the packet when the indicator changes color.
4. **Avoid the refrigerator for daily-use tea.** Refrigerators are humid, and moving tea between cold and warm air causes condensation on the leaves. Reserve refrigeration for unopened, vacuum-sealed packages intended for long-term storage.
5. **Let refrigerated tea reach room temperature before opening.** If you do store tea cold, leave the sealed package on the counter for at least one hour before breaking the seal. This prevents condensation from forming directly on the leaves.

## Choose a container

| Container type | Seal quality | Light protection | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opaque metal tin with tight lid | Good | Full | Daily-use leaf tea |
| Vacuum-sealed foil bag | Excellent | Full | Unopened long-term storage |
| Glass jar with gasket lid | Good | None (unless stored in a dark place) | Short-term display; move to a dark cupboard |
| Resealable foil pouch (original packaging) | Moderate | Partial | Finishing a package within a few weeks |

## Storage by tea style

| Style | Recommended approach |
| --- | --- |
| Steamed green tea | Store in an airtight, opaque container at room temperature. Use within four to six weeks of opening for the freshest flavor. Freeze unopened packages for storage beyond two months. |
| Roasted tea | Less sensitive to oxidation than steamed green tea. An airtight tin at room temperature is sufficient. Use within two to three months of opening. |
| Powdered tea | Highly sensitive to moisture and light. Keep in the original sealed tin or transfer to a small airtight container. Refrigerate unopened tins and bring to room temperature before opening. Use within three to four weeks once opened. |

## Signs of poor storage

- **Dull or yellowed color** in a tea that was originally bright green suggests light or heat damage.
- **Flat or papery aroma** indicates prolonged air exposure or excessive dryness.
- **Musty or off smell** points to moisture or mold from high humidity.
- **Clumped or sticky leaves** mean the tea has absorbed too much moisture. Discard the affected portion.

## Quick checklist

1. Transfer tea to an airtight, opaque container after opening.
2. Store in a cool, dark, dry spot away from heat sources and strong odors.
3. Confirm humidity stays between 40 % and 60 % with a hygrometer.
4. Reseal the container immediately after measuring out your tea.
5. Label the container with the tea style and the date you opened it.
