## Ordering and shipping

One of the most frequent questions is whether teaCapital ships internationally. The fictional brand delivers to select destinations outside its home market, though international orders may take seven to fourteen business days and are subject to local customs duties. Tracking information is emailed once the order leaves the warehouse, and you can monitor delivery progress from your account dashboard.

Another common question involves order changes after checkout. If you need to update your shipping address or swap a tea selection, contact support within twenty-four hours of placing the order. Once the shipment has entered the fulfillment queue, changes can no longer be guaranteed — but the team will do its best to accommodate last-minute requests when inventory allows.

## Brewing and preparation

Many first-time drinkers ask whether they can reuse leaves for a second or third steep. The answer is yes for most Japanese leaf teas. The second infusion often tastes different from the first — lighter and sometimes sweeter — because different compounds extract at different rates. Shorten the steep time by about fifteen seconds on each subsequent infusion to avoid over-extraction, and use water at the same temperature as the first steep.

A related question is how matcha differs from regular green tea. Both come from the same plant, but matcha is shade-grown, steamed, dried, and stone-ground into a fine powder. Because you whisk the powder directly into water and drink the entire leaf, matcha delivers a more concentrated flavor and higher nutrient content per serving. Regular green tea is steeped and strained, so the spent leaves are discarded. See the [brewing guide](/docs/brewing-guide) for preparation details on both styles.

## Storage and freshness

Proper storage is the single biggest factor in keeping your tea tasting its best. Store opened tea in an opaque, airtight container at room temperature, away from direct sunlight, moisture, and strong-smelling foods. Under these conditions, opened tea retains peak flavor for one to three months. Unopened pouches sealed at the source remain fresh for up to twelve months.

Customers sometimes ask whether freezing tea extends its life. Freezing can preserve freshness for longer periods, but it introduces a risk of moisture damage each time the container is opened. If you choose to freeze, divide the tea into single-use portions so you only thaw what you plan to brew. Let each portion reach room temperature inside its sealed bag before opening to prevent condensation from forming on the leaves.
