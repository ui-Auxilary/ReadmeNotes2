## Gather your equipment

1. **Matcha bowl (chawan):** Use a wide bowl so you have room to whisk.
2. **Bamboo whisk (chasen):** A bamboo whisk aerates the matcha evenly. Soak the tines in warm water for a minute before use to soften them.
3. **Bamboo scoop (chashaku):** Use a scoop to measure matcha, or substitute a small spoon.
4. **Fine-mesh sieve:** Sift the matcha to break up clumps before whisking.
5. **Kettle:** Heat fresh water to 80°C / 176°F. If you don't have a thermometer, let boiled water cool for about two minutes.

## Prepare your matcha

| Parameter | Usucha (thin matcha) | Koicha (thick matcha) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matcha | 2 g (about 2 scoops) | 4 g (about 4 scoops) |
| Water | 60–70 ml | 30–40 ml |
| Water temperature | 80°C / 176°F | 80°C / 176°F |

### Usucha (thin matcha)

1. Sift 2 g of matcha into your bowl to remove clumps.
2. Pour 60–70 ml of water at 80°C / 176°F into the bowl.
3. Hold the bowl steady with one hand. With the other, whisk briskly in a zigzag (W-shaped) motion for about 15–20 seconds.
4. Lift the whisk gently from the center when the surface is covered with a fine, even layer of foam.

### Koicha (thick matcha)

1. Sift 4 g of matcha into your bowl.
2. Pour 30–40 ml of water at 80°C / 176°F into the bowl.
3. Stir slowly in a circular motion—do not whisk vigorously. Koicha should be smooth and thick, without foam.
4. The result should have a consistency similar to warm paint.

## Drink your matcha

1. **Smell the matcha** before your first sip. Notice the vegetal, slightly sweet aroma.
2. **Take a small sip** and let it coat your palate. Good matcha tastes smooth, full-bodied, and slightly sweet with an umami finish.
3. **Finish the bowl** in a few sips while the matcha is warm. Matcha settles quickly, so drink it promptly.
4. If the matcha begins to settle, swirl the bowl gently to re-mix before your next sip.

## Troubleshoot your matcha

| If your matcha tastes… | Try this next time |
| --- | --- |
| Bitter or astringent (drying) | Use cooler water (closer to 75°C / 167°F) |
| Thin or weak | Add more matcha or use less water |
| Clumpy or gritty | Sift the matcha more thoroughly before adding water |
| Flat or dull | Use fresh matcha (store it sealed in the refrigerator) and fresh water |

## Optional: keep a matcha log

Record the matcha amount, water amount, temperature, and your tasting notes. Change only one variable in the next bowl to learn how it affects the flavor.
