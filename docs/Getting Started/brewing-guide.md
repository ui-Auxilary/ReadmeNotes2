Prepare and enjoy a bowl of matcha by following a simple process of sifting, whisking, and tasting.

## Gather your equipment

1. Use a scale to measure matcha powder and water.
2. Heat fresh water in a kettle with temperature control, or let boiled water cool to approximately 80°C / 176°F before pouring.
3. Prepare a fine-mesh sifter to break up clumps in the powder.
4. Use a wide bowl (chawan) and a bamboo whisk (chasen). A ceramic bowl and a small kitchen whisk work as substitutes.
5. Keep a timer nearby so you can repeat your process accurately.

## Prepare usucha (thin matcha)

Usucha is the most common way to drink matcha. It produces a light, smooth bowl with a layer of foam on top.

| Parameter | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Matcha | 2 g (about 1 tsp) |
| Water temperature | 80°C / 176°F |
| Water | 60–70 ml |

1. Sift 2 g of matcha into your bowl to remove clumps.
2. Pour a small splash of hot water into the bowl and use the whisk to form a smooth paste.
3. Add the remaining water.
4. Whisk briskly in a zigzag motion (not circular) for about 15–20 seconds, until the surface is evenly covered with fine foam.
5. Lift the whisk from the center of the bowl to leave a clean foam surface.

## Prepare koicha (thick matcha)

Koicha uses more matcha and less water to produce a rich, syrupy consistency. Use a high-quality ceremonial-grade matcha for the best result.

| Parameter | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Matcha | 4 g (about 2 tsp) |
| Water temperature | 80°C / 176°F |
| Water | 30–40 ml |

1. Sift 4 g of matcha into your bowl.
2. Add the hot water.
3. Stir slowly in a gentle kneading motion—do not whisk vigorously. The goal is a smooth, thick paste without foam.
4. Rotate the bowl to check for unmixed powder along the sides.

## Drink your matcha

1. Hold the bowl with both hands.
2. Drink the matcha while it is warm. Matcha is best consumed promptly because the powder begins to settle after a few minutes.
3. If the powder settles between sips, swirl the bowl gently to redistribute it.
4. Finish the entire bowl in a few sips to enjoy the full flavor.

## Troubleshoot your bowl

| If your matcha tastes… | Try this next time |
| --- | --- |
| Bitter or astringent (drying) | Use cooler water or a higher-quality matcha |
| Thin or weak | Add more matcha powder |
| Clumpy or gritty | Sift the powder before adding water and whisk more thoroughly |
| Too intense | Use more water or reduce the amount of matcha |
| Flat | Use fresh water and confirm your water temperature |

## Optional: keep a brew log

Record the matcha grade, powder amount, water amount, temperature, whisking time, and your tasting notes. Change only one variable in the next bowl to learn how it affects the flavor.
