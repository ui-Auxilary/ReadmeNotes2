## Why storage matters

Tea absorbs moisture, odors, and light. Poor storage dulls flavor and shortens shelf life. Following a few guidelines keeps each cup tasting close to the original tasting notes recorded during [sourcing](/docs/japanese-tea-sourcing).

## Control humidity

Humidity is the most important storage variable for Japanese tea. Excess moisture causes tea leaves to degrade, lose flavor, and develop off-tastes.

| Humidity level | Effect on tea | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Below 40% RH | Leaves may become overly dry and brittle | Acceptable for short-term storage |
| 40–60% RH | Ideal range for preserving leaf quality | Maintain this range where possible |
| Above 60% RH | Risk of moisture absorption, mold, and stale flavor | Move tea to a drier location or use a desiccant |

### Tips for managing humidity

1. Store tea in an airtight container to isolate it from ambient humidity.
2. If you live in a humid climate, place a food-safe desiccant packet inside the storage container and replace it periodically.
3. Avoid opening the container in a steamy kitchen. Let the room air settle first.
4. Never store tea in the refrigerator or freezer without a fully sealed, moisture-proof bag—condensation when removing the tea will introduce moisture directly onto the leaves.

## Choose a storage container

| Container type | Seal quality | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opaque tin with tight lid | Good | Daily-use tea you finish within a few weeks |
| Vacuum-sealed foil bag | Excellent | Bulk tea or long-term storage |
| Glass jar with gasket lid | Good | Display, if kept away from light |
| Original resealable pouch | Varies | Short-term use before transferring to a better container |

Avoid containers that previously held strong-smelling foods. Tea absorbs odors quickly.

## Keep tea cool and dark

| Factor | Guideline |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature | Room temperature or slightly below (15–25°C / 59–77°F) |
| Light | Store away from direct sunlight and fluorescent lighting |
| Heat sources | Keep containers away from stovetops, ovens, and windows |

## Shelf life by style

These are fictional estimates for unopened, properly stored teaCapital teas.

| Style | Suggested use-by window |
| --- | --- |
| Steamed green tea | 6–12 months from lot date |
| Roasted tea | 12–18 months from lot date |
| Powdered tea | 3–6 months once opened; up to 12 months sealed |

After opening, consume the tea within a few weeks for the best flavor. Reseal the container after every use.

## Storage checklist

1. Transfer tea to an airtight, opaque container.
2. Confirm the storage area is between 40–60% relative humidity.
3. Place the container in a cool, dark spot away from heat and odors.
4. Label the container with the tea style and lot date from the [collection](/docs/tea-collection).
5. Taste periodically—if the flavor has faded, adjust your brewing baseline using the [brewing guide](/docs/brewing-guide).
