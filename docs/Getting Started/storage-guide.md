## Why storage matters

Tea absorbs moisture, odors, and light. Poor storage dulls flavor and can introduce staleness within weeks. Following a few guidelines keeps each cup tasting as intended.

## Storage conditions

Keep your tea in an environment that controls these four factors.

| Factor | Recommended range | What happens if ignored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Humidity | Below 50% relative humidity | Excess moisture causes tea to absorb water, accelerating oxidation and encouraging mold growth |
| Temperature | Cool and stable, ideally 15–25°C / 59–77°F | Heat speeds chemical breakdown and dulls delicate flavors |
| Light | Store in a dark place or opaque container | UV and visible light degrade chlorophyll and other compounds, fading color and taste |
| Air exposure | Minimize contact with open air | Oxygen accelerates oxidation and allows the tea to pick up surrounding odors |

## Control humidity

Humidity is the most common cause of tea degradation in home storage.

1. **Measure your environment.** Use a small hygrometer near your tea shelf. Aim for relative humidity below 50%.
2. **Avoid kitchens and bathrooms.** Steam from cooking and bathing raises ambient humidity around stored tea.
3. **Use airtight containers.** A sealed canister or resealable foil bag with a one-way valve limits moisture exchange.
4. **Add a desiccant packet** inside the storage container if you live in a humid climate (above 60% relative humidity). Replace the packet when it is saturated.
5. **Never return damp utensils to the container.** Use a dry spoon or scoop each time you measure tea.

## Choose a container

| Container type | Humidity protection | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tin canister with tight-fitting lid | Good | Daily-use tea you open frequently |
| Resealable foil bag with zip closure | Very good | Keeping an opened bag fresh between sessions |
| Vacuum-sealed foil bag | Excellent | Long-term storage of unopened tea |
| Glass jar | Poor (unless opaque and airtight) | Display only — light and temperature swings reduce freshness |

## Storage tips by tea style

- **Steamed green tea.** The most sensitive to humidity and heat. Store in an airtight, opaque container and consume within a few weeks of opening.
- **Roasted tea.** More forgiving than steamed green tea, but still best kept sealed and away from moisture. Consume within a few months of opening.
- **Powdered tea.** Clumps easily in humid conditions. Keep the container tightly sealed and sift the powder before use if clumping occurs.

## Signs of poor storage

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Musty or stale smell | High humidity or exposure to odors |
| Visible clumping or moisture on leaves | Excess humidity inside the container |
| Faded leaf color | Light exposure |
| Flat, lifeless flavor when brewed | Oxidation from air or heat |

If you notice any of these signs, check your storage conditions and move the tea to a more suitable container and location.
