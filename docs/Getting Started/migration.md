A guide for customers transitioning to teaCapital from another tea supplier, covering how to map your existing preferences, adapt your brewing routine, and get the most from your first teaCapital order.

## Map your current teas

Before placing your first teaCapital order, take stock of what you already drink. Write down the tea styles you buy most often — for example, a daily sencha, an evening hojicha, or a weekend matcha. Note the flavor qualities you enjoy: do you prefer a sweet, umami-forward cup, or something more brisk and astringent? This inventory becomes your map for finding equivalents in the teaCapital [tea collection](/docs/tea-collection).

teaCapital organizes its teas into three broad categories: steamed green tea, roasted tea, and powdered tea. If you currently drink a Chinese-style pan-fired green tea, the closest match is a lightly steamed sencha, though the flavor profile will lean more vegetal and less smoky. If you drink a roasted oolong, hojicha offers a similar toasty warmth but with a lighter body. Matcha maps directly — the main variable is grade (ceremonial versus culinary) and the specific cultivar used.

Once you have matched each of your current teas to a teaCapital category, narrow the list to two or three options for your first order. Starting small lets you evaluate each tea carefully rather than rushing through a large shipment.

## Translate your brewing routine

Every tea supplier provides slightly different brewing recommendations, and small differences in leaf-to-water ratio, temperature, and steep time add up to a noticeably different cup. Start by brewing your first teaCapital tea using the baseline recipe on its product card or in the [brewing guide](/docs/brewing-guide). This gives you a neutral reference point before you begin adjusting.

If the result tastes stronger or more intense than what you are used to, reduce the leaf amount by 1 g or shorten the steep by 10 seconds. If it tastes thinner, do the opposite. The goal is not to replicate your old cup exactly — teaCapital teas are sourced and processed differently — but to find a version of the new tea that fits your palate. Keep a simple brew log noting the tea name, amount, water volume, temperature, time, and your impression. Changing only one variable per brew makes it easy to identify what moved the flavor in the right direction.

Water is the variable most people overlook when switching suppliers. If your previous teas tasted fine with your tap water but the new ones seem flat or chalky, test your water's TDS level. teaCapital teas are developed with soft or filtered water (50–80 ppm) in mind, so a simple carbon filter may be all you need to close the gap.

## Place and evaluate your first order

With your shortlist and baseline recipes ready, place an order for two to three teas. When the package arrives, brew each tea once using the recommended baseline before making any changes. Taste them at the suggested serving temperature (60–70°C for green teas) and note your first impressions: aroma, sweetness, bitterness, body, and finish.

If you still have stock from your previous supplier, brew the old and new teas side by side using the same water and cup. A direct comparison highlights the differences more clearly than memory alone. Pay attention to which qualities you prefer in each cup — you may find that the teaCapital version excels in umami but needs a cooler brew temperature to match the sweetness you are accustomed to. Record these observations so you can refine your recipe on the next brew.

After two or three sessions with each tea, you will have enough data to decide which teas earn a permanent spot in your rotation and which need further adjustment. This is also a good time to explore the [subscription](/docs/subscription) plans if you want a recurring delivery schedule.

## Complete the transition

Once you have identified your preferred teas and dialed in your recipes, consolidate your orders with teaCapital. Moving to a single supplier simplifies inventory management and gives you access to consistent lot tracking — every tea is tied to a specific batch with documented tasting notes and brewing baselines. If a lot changes between orders, the product page reflects the update so you can adjust your recipe accordingly.

Keep your brew log active even after the transition is complete. Seasonal variation, different harvest years, and new lots mean that the same tea name may taste slightly different from one order to the next. A running log helps you spot these shifts early and adapt your brewing rather than wondering why the cup changed. Over time, the log becomes a personal reference that makes every new teaCapital tea easier to dial in.
