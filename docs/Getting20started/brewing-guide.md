## Gather your equipment

1. Use a scale to measure tea and water — eyeballing works in a pinch, but a scale keeps things consistent.
2. Heat fresh water in a kettle with temperature control, or let boiled water cool before pouring.
3. Prepare an infuser or teapot for leaf tea. Use a bowl and whisk for powdered tea.
4. Keep a timer nearby so you can repeat a recipe accurately.

## Boil your water

Start with fresh, cold water each time you brew. Reboiled water loses dissolved oxygen and can make tea taste flat.

1. Fill your kettle with cold, filtered water. Avoid distilled water — a small amount of mineral content actually improves flavor.
2. Bring the water to a full, rolling boil (100°C / 212°F).
3. If your recipe calls for a lower temperature, let the boiled water cool in the kettle or pour it into a separate vessel to speed things up. As a rough guide, water loses about 10°C for every minute it sits off the heat in an open kettle.
4. Use a thermometer or a kettle with a temperature display for accuracy. Small differences in temperature can noticeably change the taste of green and powdered teas.

<Callout icon="💡" theme="info">
  **Quick cool trick:** Pouring boiled water from one vessel into another drops the temperature by roughly 5–10°C per transfer. Give it a try!
</Callout>

## Brew leaf tea

Here's a great starting point for the fictional steamed green and roasted tea styles.

| Parameter | Steamed green tea | Roasted tea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tea | 5 g | 5 g |
| Water | 200 ml | 200 ml |
| Water temperature | 70°C / 158°F | 90°C / 194°F |
| First steep | 60 seconds | 60 seconds |

1. Measure the tea into your infuser or teapot.
2. Measure and heat the water to the listed temperature.
3. Pour the water over the leaves and start the timer.
4. Pour the entire infusion into your cup when the timer ends. Fully decanting (pouring out all liquid) prevents the leaves from continuing to steep.
5. Take a sip while it's warm and enjoy!

## Whisk powdered tea

1. Sift 2 g of powdered tea into a bowl to break up clumps.
2. Add 60 ml of water at 80°C / 176°F.
3. Whisk briskly in a zigzag motion for about 20 seconds, until the surface looks evenly mixed and lightly foamy.
4. Drink promptly — and give the bowl a swirl between sips if powder begins to settle.

## Brew cold tea

Cold brewing extracts flavor slowly with room-temperature or chilled water, producing a smoother, naturally sweeter cup with less bitterness. It's perfect for warm days!

| Parameter | Cold-brew leaf tea |
| --- | --- |
| Tea | 10 g |
| Water | 500 ml (room temperature or cold) |
| Steep time | 6–8 hours in the refrigerator |

1. Place the tea leaves in a pitcher or bottle with a built-in strainer.
2. Pour room-temperature or cold water over the leaves.
3. Seal the container and refrigerate for 6–8 hours, or overnight.
4. Strain the leaves and serve over ice if you like.
5. Drink within 24 hours for the best flavor.

Steamed green tea works especially well cold-brewed; roasted tea produces a lighter, toasty iced drink that's worth trying too.

## Troubleshoot the cup

Don't worry if your first cup isn't perfect — that's all part of the fun. Here's how to dial it in:

| If your tea tastes… | Try this next time |
| --- | --- |
| Bitter or astringent (drying) | Use cooler water or steep for less time |
| Thin or weak | Add more tea or steep slightly longer |
| Too intense | Use less tea or add more water |
| Flat | Use fresh water and confirm your water temperature |

## Optional: keep a brew log

Recording your tea style, tea amount, water amount, temperature, time, and tasting notes is a great way to learn what you like. Change only one variable in the next brew to see how it affects the cup.
